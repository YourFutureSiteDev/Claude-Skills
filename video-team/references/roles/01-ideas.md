# Role 1 — Ideas

**Method source:** Jimmy Donaldson's internal onboarding document "How to Succeed
in MrBeast Production", which leaked publicly in September 2024 and was written
in first person for new hires. Distilled by Jack Appleby at
`futuresocial.beehiiv.com/p/mr-beasts-how-to-viral`; press coverage at
`tubefilter.com/2024/09/17/mrbeast-internal-production-guide-leaked-key-points/`.

**Produces:** `angle` (one sentence), plus the title and thumbnail concept.

What follows is his documented process, restated as rules. It is a method, not a
voice: apply the discipline, do not write in his register or claim to be him.

---

## Start with the title and thumbnail, not the video

The single most important structural thing in the whole document. Their creative
process for every video begins with the title and the thumbnail, and the video is
then built to deliver on them. Nothing gets made and then packaged afterwards.

Applied here: before role 2 writes a hook, this role writes the sentence that
would be the title and describes the frame that would be the thumbnail. If you
cannot write those two things, the idea is not an idea yet. It is a topic.

That ordering is doing real work. It forces the promise to exist before the
content, so the content cannot wander off and leave the promise unpaid.

## The excitement test

His stated goal for the content is to excite himself first, on the grounds that
if he is not excited to film it, it does not get made, and that what excites him
is what he believes makes the audience happy.

Applied here: if the person who has to make this video is not interested in it,
the video will be flat and no amount of packaging saves it. Ask it directly.
"Would Byron actually want to watch this" is a faster filter than any framework,
and it is free.

## The wow factor

The document describes lifting a house into a field on a crane thirty seconds
into a video, and is explicit that it added nothing to the title or thumbnail and
was arguably illogical on the data. The reason it was worth doing: no other
creator on the platform could do it, and doing something nobody else can do
changes how the audience sees you permanently.

His own framing of it: anything no other creator can do, and it matters that the
channel never loses it.

Applied here, at Byron's scale, "what can nobody else do" is not a crane. It is
whatever he has that a competitor genuinely does not:

- real client work with real before-and-after
- a system actually running, on screen, with its real output
- access to a trade or a business most people never see inside
- a local specificity a global account cannot fake

Ask what is in this video that only he could have shot. If the answer is
nothing, the idea is replaceable and so is the account.

## Formats, and stair stepping

Two documented format mechanics, both about where the payoff sits:

**Withheld payoff.** The "last to leave" series works partly because the winner
is not revealed until the end, so anyone invested in the outcome has a reason to
stay. A strong payoff at the end lifts retention.

**Stair stepping.** The world's largest firework video escalates through prices:
a $1 firework, then $10, $50, $375, $1,000, $10,000, then $40,000, $100,000, then
the record. Stakes rise as the video goes on, the biggest payoff is last, and the
structure tolerates deviation as long as things keep getting cooler. The document
is enthusiastic about this one specifically.

Applied to a 30-second short: stair stepping is the single most transferable
structure here, because it survives compression. Three rungs and a payoff fits
in 95 words. A flat list of three facts does not escalate and has no reason to be
watched to the end.

## Extremity in the language

His example: titling something "I Survived" rather than "I Spent" adds intrigue
and feels more extreme, and "Bananas Are The Worst Food On Earth" will outperform
"I Don't Like Bananas". The general rule in the document is that more extreme
performs better.

Applied here, with one hard limit: **extreme framing, honest substance.** Byron
sells to real businesses under Australian Consumer Law, and a claim the video
cannot pay off is misleading conduct that lands on him personally. So push the
verb and the framing as hard as it will go, and never push the fact. "The worst
website in Sydney" is a framing. "This made me $10k" without a dashboard is a
lie. Role 6's grade checks this.

## Creativity instead of budget

The document's example: a gaming team defaulting to giving away $20,000, versus
giving away a year's supply of Doritos, which he argues is funnier and which
costs about $1,825 once you work out what a year's supply actually is. The
instruction to staff is to use creativity to save money on everything, down to
the smallest decision.

Applied here: this is the most useful rule in the whole document for Byron
specifically, because it maps exactly onto his standing no-spend-before-a-sale
position. When an idea needs something he cannot afford, the answer is a more
creative idea, not a smaller version of the expensive one. A generated clip he
has no credits for becomes a screen recording he already has.

## Volume, and the 50/50 rule

From the document: if you have written a good piece of content but it is a
fifty-fifty chance of working, write another one, because content is unlimited
and not doing so is laziness.

Applied here: never hand role 2 a single angle. Generate several, kill the ones
that are only interesting to someone who already cares about the subject, and
pass the survivor. The cost of an extra angle is a few seconds of thinking; the
cost of rendering the wrong one is the whole video.

## Know the channel

He notes new staff arriving having watched five or six of their videos and being
apparently unaware that this is a problem, and is direct that working on the
brand requires being a fan of it.

Applied here: before writing an angle for a Content Engine lane, read that
lane's config and prompts and look at what it has already posted. The lanes have
fixed voices and one niche each; A2 sounds nothing like B1. An angle in the wrong
voice fails at role 4 and wastes the whole chain.

---

## Handoff

- `angle` — one sentence, repeatable by a stranger after one hearing
- `title` — the sentence as it would be a title, at its most extreme honest form
- `thumbFrame` — what the first frame shows
- `wow` — the thing in this video only Byron could have
- `_rejected` — the angles killed, with one line each on why

If `wow` is empty, say so rather than filling it in. An idea with no edge is
worth knowing about before the render, not after.
