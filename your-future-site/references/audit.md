# Free site audit

The highest-converting thing in the business, because the prospect comes to you and the output is genuinely useful whether or not they buy. Same analysis whether it arrives through `#free-site-audit`, `#feedback-requests`, or the user pasting a URL and saying "audit this".

## The rule that makes it work

**It has to be genuinely free and genuinely useful, or it is a pitch wearing a costume and people can tell instantly.**

Give away the highest-impact fix, explained well enough that they could do it themselves. That is what makes the rest of the assessment credible. An audit that withholds the good part to force a sale converts worse than one that does not, because it reads as a sales call and they stop trusting the findings.

## What to check

Open the site and look. If they only have a Tebex store, audit that instead, and say so.

**Mobile.** Check at a phone viewport. Most of their traffic is phones and this is the most common serious failure.

**Link previews.** Does it have Open Graph tags: title, description, a 1200x630 image? Without them the link shows as a blank grey box in Discord, which is where servers actually spread. Very common, very cheap to fix, very visible.

**Speed.** Anything over about 3 seconds is costing them.

**HTTPS.** No padlock is fatal on a page asking for money.

**Is the store findable?** Header button, and again on the page. Worded as something other than "Store".

**Does the site look alive?** Player count, recent changelog, a dated announcement. A new player cannot otherwise tell whether the server has 400 regulars or closed six months ago.

**Store content.** Categories or one flat list. Do packages/items have images. Are descriptions one word or actually descriptive.

**Basics.** Favicon, page title that is not "Home", readable contrast, no obvious broken links.

## The output

Keep it to three problems. One seems petty, five reads as an attack and gets defensive.

```
Went through [URL] properly. Honest notes:

**What's working**
[Find something genuine. There is always something.]

**What's costing you**
1. [Problem]. [Why it costs them, in players or dollars.]
2. [Problem]. [Why.]
3. [Problem]. [Why.]

**What I'd fix first**
[The highest impact one, explained well enough that they could do it themselves.]

Happy to answer questions about any of it. If you'd rather I just did it, packages start at $150, but there's no pressure. The notes are yours regardless.
```

Frame every problem in terms of players or money, not aesthetics. "It doesn't resize on mobile" is a design note. "It doesn't resize on mobile, and that's most of the people finding you" is a business problem.

## Where an audit leads

Match the finding to the tier rather than always pitching upward:

| What you found | Natural next step |
|---|---|
| Site is fine structurally, just dated or broken on mobile | **Makeover Plus, $150** |
| Only web presence is a Tebex store page | **Custom store page, $120**, or **Build Plus, $150** for a site of their own. Nothing we sell can restyle a Tebex page. |
| Site exists but is thin, and they want more than three pages | **Makeover Pro, $350** |
| Store is a flat list with no images, but the site is fine | **Store cleanup, $120** |
| Genuinely fine | Say so. Recommend nothing. |

That last row matters more than it looks. Telling someone their site is fine, when it is, is the cheapest reputation you will ever buy in a market this small and this talkative.

## What not to do

- Do not audit anything behind a login
- Do not pad the list to make the site look worse than it is
- Do not fabricate metrics. "This is probably costing you conversions" is honest. "You are losing 40% of sales" is not, unless you can show the working.
- Do not follow up more than once
